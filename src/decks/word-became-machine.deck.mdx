## Hi, I'm Ben

![bg right:40%](./assets/word-became-machine/headshot-ben.avif)

AI researcher for **20 years**

Christian **my whole life**

these days: **language models**, building software with them, and teaching how
they work

```notes
Twenty years in AI and machine learning research, at the ANU. A Christian for longer than that --- my whole life.

What I work on now is language models: what they're good for, especially in software development, which is where I use them all day. And how to teach both experts and complete novices how they actually work, because we can't have a decent argument about the opportunities and the dangers of a thing we can't see inside.

So that's the plan: first we look inside one, together. Then I want to tell you what it's like to work with them.
```

## one chapter

{/* prettier-ignore */}
<p class="corpus-quote fragment">“From the first he was the Word, and the Word was in relation with God and was God.”<br /><span class="corpus-attribution">John 1:1, Bible in Basic English (1949)</span></p>
<p class="corpus-quote fragment">“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”<br /><span class="corpus-attribution">John 1:1, King James Version</span></p>

```notes
Click the first in and read it out. Let the room hear it as an unfamiliar sentence for a beat before the second click lands the one they know.

The Bible in Basic English: S. H. Hooke's translation, New Testament 1941, whole Bible 1949, in Ogden's Basic English --- 850 words, plus 150 for poetry and the specifically biblical vocabulary. A thousand words to say the whole of scripture. The most famous prologue in the canon, and most of this room didn't recognise it on the sheets because every word had been swapped for a plainer one.

That's the whole corpus: John 1, all fifty-one verses, one translation. Nothing else went in.
```

## between us, we have the whole chapter

![bg right:40%](./assets/word-became-machine/bg-participants.avif)

**1,267** tokens · **263** distinct · **1,266** token pairs dealt across **22**
sheets

no single sheet has the whole picture

```notes
Every one of the 1,266 word pairs in the chapter was dealt to exactly one sheet. So the model wasn't on my laptop and it wasn't on anyone's page --- for the last five minutes it existed only across the whole room. That's not a metaphor for how a model is distributed. It's literally where the model was.

The ones you use are the same kind of thing, a billion times over, looking back over more than one word, and trained on very nearly everything anyone has written down. The loop is the same: look at the context, weigh the options, pick one, write it down.

That's how it works. I don't have conclusions about what it means. What I have is what it's like to work with one all day.
```

I build software now\
by **describing** it

![bg](./assets/word-became-machine/bg-word-machine.avif)

```notes
The website this talk lives on, and the tool that printed your sheets, I built mostly by talking to one of these. I've been writing code for twenty years, in languages where every character is an instruction and the machine does exactly and only what you typed. Now I say what I want, in English, and something interprets it, makes decisions, and builds. I find myself explaining, correcting, trusting, supervising.

The ones I use are the thing we just ran, a billion times over, and wired to tools: what they write runs code, changes files, sends things. Text has started to act.

I don't know what to make of that. But I keep noticing that the people in this room belong to a tradition in which a word that does things is not a strange idea. So: four verses I've been reading alongside the work. No conclusions. Just what it feels like.
```

{/* _class: hero */}

“And God said, Let there be light:\
**and there was light**”

<span class="verse-ref">Genesis 1:3, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/word-became-machine/bg-witness.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

The feeling: I say "let there be a login page" and there is one. Nothing in twenty years of programming felt like that; it used to be bricklaying. Ps 33:9 in the BBE is closer still to the experience: "he gave the word, and it was done".

The distance: mine isn't from nothing. It's speech into a world already made, assembled from what millions of other people built, and what comes back is often not quite what I meant. There's a gap between my intention and its fulfilment that Genesis 1 doesn't have.

The honest question: what is it that feels powerful --- being understood, being obeyed, or no longer having to understand every step myself? And does this deepen my wonder at "and there was light", or make it feel deceptively familiar?
```

---

{/* _class: hero */}

“I say to this one, Go, and he goes ...\
and to my servant, **Do this, and he does it**”

<span class="verse-ref">Matthew 8:9, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/word-became-machine/bg-narrative-shaped.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

The centurion, asking Jesus to heal his servant: "only say the word". He recognises Jesus' authority because he knows from the inside what it is to give an order and have it carried out somewhere he isn't.

The feeling: this is my working day now. I don't write the code; I send. Go, come, do this.

What the verse keeps in view that I lose: "I myself am a man under authority." He gives orders as someone who answers to someone. When the agent does what I said, what am I exercising authority over? And the ease of saying "do this" --- does it change how much of the result I feel is mine to answer for?
```

---

{/* _class: hero */}

“Give your servant, then, a wise heart ...\
able to see **what is good and what evil**”

<span class="verse-ref">1 Kings 3:9, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/word-became-machine/bg-hall-generation-wide.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

Solomon has just been handed more scope for action than he knows what to do with --- "I am only a young boy, with no knowledge of how to go out or come in" (v7). Offered anything, he doesn't ask for more capability. He already has that. He asks for judgement.

The feeling: building used to be the hard part, and the difficulty did some of my deciding for me. Most bad ideas died because they weren't worth the month. Now the month is an afternoon, and nothing dies of effort. What's left is only the question of whether it should exist.

Which decisions get harder when making gets easier? What would discernment look like before I issue the next instruction, rather than after?
```

---

{/* _class: hero */}

“And so the Word became flesh\
and **took a place among us** for a time”

<span class="verse-ref">John 1:14, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/word-became-machine/bg-word-flesh.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

The verse you were holding this afternoon, in pieces, in the translation you were holding it in.

Where the thread arrives. God speaks and there is light; his word goes out and does what he sent it for; and then the Word doesn't send anything. He comes. One body, one town, one span of years. "Took a place among us for a time."

The feeling: my words reach further every month, and I can be in fewer places while more gets done. The direction of John 1:14 is the other way --- toward being somewhere, with someone, at cost. Later in the same gospel, Jesus, "being conscious that the Father had put everything into his hands", gets up from the table and washes feet (13:3--5). Everything in his hands, and that's what he does with them.

I'm learning to speak and have things made. What I don't want to lose track of is what I'm learning to love while I do it. That's where I've got to. I'd like to know what you make of it.
```
