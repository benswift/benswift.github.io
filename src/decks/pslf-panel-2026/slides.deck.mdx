```notes
- School of Cybernetics; livecoding; fifteen years teaching programming, now teaching agentic coding (students + LLMs Unplugged this arvo)
- my angle: not what the models can do, what changes about the _work_
- pick up from Lexing's adoption curve, zoom in on one segment
- the IR deal: it made _pretty good_ cheap. Tool up once, stamp out thousands. The price was fit: the factory's sizes, not the tailor's cut
- software ran the same deal, harder: the _first copy_ is the whole cost. So SaaS: sell it ten thousand times, everyone bends their process to fit
- this room's software estate is off the rack all the way down. It was the only deal on offer
```

```notes
- agentic coding collapses the cost of the first copy: idea to working thing in an afternoon
- bespoke at the off-the-shelf price. This revolution runs the IR deal the other way: _exactly right_, cheap
- precise: the first working version gets cheap, not "software"
- but a tailor needs a tape measure: calendars, mail, files are where this place's real processes live
```

---

{/* _class: hero */}

![bg](./assets/bg-personal-shopper.avif)

```notes
- concede it: ten thousand hand-made tools is a real maintenance problem, and caution about agents near our data is fair
- but mind the gap between "embrace AI" and what lands on the desktop. We were promised a tailor and mostly got a personal shopper
- helpful, friendly, knows the stock --- and it can only take you back to the rack. No agentic tools, no access to the data, nothing it can measure or cut
- a personal shopper helps you choose what fits best. A tailor makes it fit
- all the enthusiasm, none of the agency
```

---

{/* _class: hero */}

![bg](./assets/bg-the-crit.avif)

```notes
- the opportunity if we hand over the tape measure: more software, made by people who know how their unit works. Home-cooked software; a chance to make good software again
- making was the bottleneck; now the constraint is knowing what you want, and saying why. Those people are in this room, not the IT shop
- govern with judgement, not prohibition. It's a crit: pin it up, say why this one fits
- a choice you can't argue for is a default --- and that cuts both ways, for what we switch on and what we switch off
```

```notes
- closer: if you think AI is going to change the world, but you can't imagine it changing your default office productivity suite, you've got a much narrower idea of "changing the world" than you think
- a personal shopper helps you find your way around that suite; a tailor changes it
- (backup if needed: Satya Nadella, BG2 podcast, Dec 2024 --- business apps "will all collapse in the agent era", and "why do I need Excel?" His answer: the agent drives Excel for you. Only works if the agent is allowed to touch it)
```

```comment
Q&A hooks: Jevons / ten-thousand-tools; the security case for locking agents out
(data exfiltration, privacy, compliance) --- agree it's real, the ask is scoped
access and judgement rather than "open everything"; the sharper version of the
metaphor is a tailor with no scissors (agentic tools) and no tape measure (Graph
access); Pine's mass customisation (1993) and Anderson's _Makers_ (2012) promised
this for atoms and fizzled, because you can't collapse the fixed cost of a
physical thing --- you can for bits; Sloan and Appleton's "home-cooked software"
for the tailor-vs-factory framing.
```
