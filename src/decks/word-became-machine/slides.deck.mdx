## Hi, I'm Ben

![bg right:40%](./assets/headshot-ben.avif)

AI researcher for **20 years**

Christian **my whole life**

these days: **language models**, building software with them, and teaching how
they work

```notes
Twenty years in AI and machine learning research, at the ANU. A Christian for longer than that --- my whole life.

What I work on now is language models: what they're good for, especially in software development, which is where I use them all day. And how to teach both experts and complete novices how they actually work, because we can't have a decent argument about the opportunities and the dangers of a thing we can't see inside.

So that's the plan: first we look inside one, together. Then I want to tell you what it's like to work with them.
```

```notes
Click the first in and read it out. Let them hear it as an unfamiliar sentence for a beat before the second click lands the one they know.

The Bible in Basic English: S. H. Hooke's translation, New Testament 1941, whole Bible 1949, in Ogden's Basic English --- 850 words, plus 150 for poetry and the specifically biblical vocabulary. A thousand words to say the whole of scripture. The most famous prologue in the canon, and most of you didn't recognise it on the sheets: partly because the pairs were shuffled, and partly because Hooke had swapped the familiar words for plainer ones.

That's the whole corpus: John 1, all fifty-one verses, one translation. Nothing else went in.
```

## between us, we have the whole chapter

![bg right:40%](./assets/bg-participants.avif)

**1,267** tokens · **263** distinct · **1,266** token pairs dealt across **22**
sheets

no single sheet has the whole picture

```notes
Every one of the 1,266 word pairs in the chapter was dealt to exactly one sheet. So the model wasn't on my laptop and it wasn't on anyone's page --- for the last five minutes it existed only across all of you. That's literally where the model was.

The ones you use are the same kind of thing, a billion times over, looking back over more than one word, and trained on very nearly everything anyone has written down. The loop is the same: look at the context, weigh the options, pick one, write it down.

That's how it works. I don't have conclusions about what it means. What I have is what it's like to work with one all day.
```

{/* _class: hero */}

I build software now\
by **describing** it

![bg](./assets/bg-word-machine.avif)

```notes
The website this talk lives on, and the tool that printed your sheets, I built mostly by talking to one of these. I've been writing code for twenty years, in languages where every character is an instruction and the machine does exactly and only what you typed. Now I say what I want, in English, and something interprets it, makes decisions, and builds. I find myself explaining, correcting, trusting, supervising.

The ones I use run that same loop, and they're wired to tools: what they write runs code, changes files, sends things.

It feels like managing people. The centurion would recognise it: "I say to this one, Go, and he goes." But giving orders to people is as old as people. The strange part is that these aren't people. What they are, and whether they're intelligent, is a live argument and I'm not going to settle it from up here. What I can tell you is that this is the first time in my life I've spoken to a part of creation that isn't human and had it do what I said.

Scripture has a lot of words spoken to things that aren't people. Almost all of them are God's. So here are five verses I've been reading alongside the work, and what the work has felt like next to them.
```

{/* _class: hero */}

“And God said, Let there be light:\
**and there was light**”

<span class="verse-ref">Genesis 1:3, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/bg-witness.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

The baseline. The first word in scripture is spoken to no one. There's nobody there to hear it and obey; it's addressed to what isn't anyone, and it happens. Ps 33:9: "he gave the word, and it was done". Ps 148:8: "storm-wind, doing his word". In this book, things that aren't people doing what they're told is what God's speech is like.

The feeling: I say "let there be a login page" and there is one. Nothing in twenty years of programming felt like that; it used to be bricklaying. For twenty years the furthest my words reached into the non-human world was Adam's share: I could name things (Gen 2:19). Now I name a thing and it gets built.

The distance: mine isn't from nothing. It's speech into a world already made, assembled from what millions of other people wrote, and what comes back is often not quite what I meant. Does this deepen my wonder at "and there was light", or make it feel deceptively familiar?
```

{/* _class: hero */}

“Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea\
**do his orders?**”

<span class="verse-ref">Mark 4:41, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/bg-wind-sea.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

He "gave strong orders to the wind, and said to the sea, Peace, be at rest." And the disciples' response isn't relief. "Their fear was great." They're more afraid after the storm than during it, because everyone in that boat knows whose orders the sea takes.

The feeling: the first time one of these did what I asked --- properly, a whole working thing from a paragraph of English --- what I felt wasn't quite delight. It was closer to the boat. Things that aren't people aren't supposed to do what they're told.

I've mostly lost that. It's a Tuesday now. I'm not sure losing it is a good sign. What would it be to keep some of the disciples' fear?
```

---

{/* _class: hero */}

“And Balaam said to the ass,\
**You have made me seem foolish**”

<span class="verse-ref">Numbers 22:29, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/bg-donkey.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

Tell it for the laugh. Balaam's donkey stops three times; he beats her three times; "the Lord gave the ass the power of talking", and she asks what she's done to deserve it. And Balaam answers her. He doesn't fall off. He doesn't say "you can talk?" He's straight into the argument: you've made me look stupid, if I had a sword I'd kill you.

The feeling: that's me by about the second week. The first time it talked back I was amazed. Now I argue with it. I've been short with it. I have, more or less, typed Balaam's line. How fast a conversation with something that isn't human stopped being remarkable --- that's the thing I'd have got most wrong if you'd asked me five years ago.

Leave hanging: she answers him quite reasonably --- "have I ever done this to you before?" "No." And in the story, the one who was sure he was the clever half of the conversation was the one who couldn't see what was standing in the road.
```

---

{/* _class: hero */}

“are we to get water for you\
**out of the rock?**”

<span class="verse-ref">Numbers 20:10, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/bg-rock.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

Moses is told to "give orders to the rock" before the people's eyes (v8), and it will give water. A human voice, a non-human thing, by God's gift. What he does instead: "are we to get water for you out of the rock?" --- and hits it twice with the rod. The water comes anyway. And it costs him the promised land.

Two failures, and I recognise both. He reaches for force where he was given a word: the old tool, the one that worked last time (Exodus 17). And he says "we". He speaks as though the power were his.

The feeling: it works whether or not I've got my heart right. The water came out. Nothing in the tool will tell me when I've started saying "we". Which decisions get harder when making gets easier?
```

{/* _class: hero */}

“And so the Word became flesh\
and **took a place among us** for a time”

<span class="verse-ref">John 1:14, BBE</span>

![bg](./assets/bg-word-flesh.avif)

```notes
Riff, don't recite. Prompts:

The verse you were holding earlier, in pieces, in the translation you were holding it in.

God speaks and there is light; he gives the word and it is done; wind and sea do his orders. And then the Word doesn't send anything. He comes. One body, one town, one span of years. "Took a place among us for a time."

The feeling: my words reach further every month, and I can be in fewer places while more gets done. The direction of John 1:14 is the other way --- toward being somewhere, with someone, at cost. Later in the same gospel, Jesus, "being conscious that the Father had put everything into his hands", gets up from the table and washes feet (13:3--5). Everything in his hands, and that's what he does with them.

I'm learning to speak and have things made. What I don't want to lose track of is what I'm learning to love while I do it. That's where I've got to. I'd like to know what you make of it.
```
