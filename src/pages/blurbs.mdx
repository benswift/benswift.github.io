Here's a list of bios/blurbs that you could use in your glossy brochures. I'm
open to being described/introduced in new ways, too---although if you're going
to do that then I'd appreciate having a discussion with you about it first :)

## General audience (short)

Dr. Ben Swift is an educator and artist-programmer who builds things that make
AI legible to the people it affects. He's a Senior Lecturer at the ANU School of
Cybernetics, where he leads the _Cybernetic Studio_. The Studio's Human-Scale AI
program slows computation down and blows it up until people can get their hands
on it. Its works include language models built with pen, paper and dice, and a
neural network you operate by hand.

## Academic (short)

Dr. Ben Swift is an interdisciplinary scholar and artist-programmer working on
AI literacy, agentic software development and the cultural life of machine
learning. He's a Senior Lecturer at the ANU School of Cybernetics, where he
leads the _Cybernetic Studio_ and its Human-Scale AI program, which makes
machine learning legible by building versions of it that people can handle and
take apart. He created [LLMs Unplugged](https://llmsunplugged.org), a workshop
where learners build language models with pen, paper and dice. He also created
[_COMP4020 Agentic Coding Studio_](https://comp.anu.edu.au/courses/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/),
an ANU course on building software with LLM-based coding agents.

## Academic (long)

Dr. Ben Swift is an internationally-recognised interdisciplinary
[scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=OQdYgLEAAAAJ), artist and
[creative technologist/software engineer](https://github.com/benswift). He's an
expert in AI/machine learning, computing education, cybernetics, full-stack web
development and computational art & music.

He leads the
[_Cybernetic Studio_](https://cybernetics.anu.edu.au/cybernetic-studio/), the
creative practice arm of the
[School of Cybernetics](https://cybernetics.anu.edu.au/) at the
[Australian National University](https://anu.edu.au). Its Human-Scale AI program
slows computation down and blows it up until people can see and handle it. The
best-known result is [LLMs Unplugged](https://llmsunplugged.org), a hands-on
workshop where learners build language models with pen, paper and dice. It has
reached thousands of participants, from primary school students to senior
government executives. Other Studio works include the _Perceptron Apparatus_, a
1.2-metre wooden device for performing neural network calculations by hand. In
_PANIC!_, an interactive installation, visitors watch meaning drift as it passes
through feedback loops of generative AI models. He also convenes
[_COMP4020 Agentic Coding Studio_](https://comp.anu.edu.au/courses/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/),
an ANU course where students build software with LLM-based coding agents and
critique it in weekly studio sessions.

Some of the Studio's work turns the same question on the institutions that run
AI. The [_APS AI Tracker_](https://apsaitracker.app), an open-data project,
keeps a daily, version-controlled archive of every Australian Government
agency's AI transparency statement. Its public site shows how each statement
changes over time, and how much of it was copied from the government's own
template. [_Slop University_](https://slop.university) presses a fictional
university's visual identity over documents both real and fabricated, until the
genuine and the invented are hard to tell apart.

Ben's interest in generative systems long predates the current AI boom. As a
[livecoding artist](/livecoding/) he has spent more than fifteen years writing
music-making code in front of audiences. He has performed at the
[International Symposium on Electronic Art](https://www.isea-web.org) (ISEA) and
the [International Conference on Live Coding](https://iclc.toplap.org) (ICLC).
He works in the open, with nearly all of his research code and teaching
materials on GitHub (on his [personal account](https://github.com/benswift) and
the [ANU Cybernetics organisation](https://github.com/ANUcybernetics)). He has
more than 15 years' experience as an OSS community manager, technical writer and
software engineer.

## Academic (educator)

Dr. Ben Swift is an interdisciplinary scholar and artist-programmer with
interests and expertise in AI/machine learning, cybernetics, data vis/data
science, human-computer interaction and computational art & music. He has
extensive learning and teaching experience in a wide range of contexts, having
worked across two schools at the ANU: the
[School of Computing](https://comp.anu.edu.au) (2012--2021) and the
[School of Cybernetics](https://cybernetics.anu.edu.au) (2021--present). He has
designed, convened and taught large courses (e.g. 400+ student core COMP
courses), innovative cross-campus electives (e.g. the ANU Laptop Ensemble), and
extension courses (e.g. the ANU Extension "Creative Computing" H-course). He has
been recognised for his teaching with a 2018 Vice Chancellor's Citation for
Outstanding Contribution to Student Learning. More recently, he created
[LLMs Unplugged](https://llmsunplugged.org), a hands-on workshop where learners
build language models with pen, paper and dice. It has been delivered to
thousands of participants across schools, universities and government. He also
convenes
[_COMP4020 Agentic Coding Studio_](https://comp.anu.edu.au/courses/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/),
which uses studio-style weekly critique sessions to teach students to build
software with LLM-based AI agents.

He is a previous Associate Director (Education) and team leader for "Exec Ed"
Learning Experiences at the School of Cybernetics. He currently leads the
_Cybernetic Studio_, the School's creative practice arm, whose Human-Scale AI
program builds versions of machine learning that people can see and handle.

## Government and industry

Dr. Ben Swift helps public servants and executives understand how today's AI
systems actually work. He's a Senior Lecturer at the ANU School of Cybernetics,
where he leads the _Cybernetic Studio_. In his
[LLMs Unplugged](https://llmsunplugged.org) workshop, participants build a
working language model with pen, paper and dice. It has been delivered to senior
public servants and industry executives as well as schools and universities. He
also builds open-data tools for AI governance, including the
[APS AI Tracker](https://apsaitracker.app), a public archive of every Australian
Government agency's AI transparency statement. After more than twenty years as a
software developer, he now teaches ANU students to build software with AI coding
agents. His account of what these tools can and can't do comes from using them
every day.

## Livecoder (short, informal)

Dr. Ben Swift is a livecoder and artist-programmer with a day job in academia.
The day job mostly wins out over his longing to escape into the woods with a
laptop and a solar panel to make beats with code. He's a long-time contributor
to the [Extempore](https://github.com/digego/extempore) programming environment,
and has performed with Ushini Attanayake as _Funktion_
([videos](https://vimeo.com/benswift)).

## Explanation of livecoding (general audience)

'Livecoding' is the act of programming (writing code) as a musical
performance---in front of an audience. You can see the code being written in
front of your eyes, and this code is making _all_ the music that you can hear.

It's kindof like conducting an orchestra; but instead of humans in tuxedoes, the
'musicians' are the little bits of code that you can see being written
on-the-fly. They follow the instructions they're given, they play the notes
they're told to, and they never hit bum notes! They're an army of musical robot
ninjas summoned into existence in the improvisational performance of live
coding.

So swing by and try to figure out what's going on; try and understand which
little bits of code are triggering which sounds and notice the way that the
music changes as the code evolves. Or if it all seems too complicated then just
sit back, watch the pretty colours and enjoy listening to the robot ninjas as
they play diligently and tirelessly for your listening pleasure.
