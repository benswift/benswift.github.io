**Research background.** Large language models are widely used and little
understood. Their statistical machinery is hidden behind a chat box: training
as counting, generation as weighted sampling. _LLMs Unplugged_ works in
computing education and public engagement. It asks how that machinery can be
made tangible enough to hold, and whether human scale changes how people
reason about these models.

**Research contribution.** The project is a family of physical artefacts that
bring the statistics down to human scale. The _LLM Library_ is a growing shelf
of pre-trained models in hardbound print. Each volume typesets the n-gram
frequency tables of a single literary work, in bigram, trigram and 4-gram
editions. A reader can hold a whole model in their hands and generate new text
with a die. _Model Citizens_ gathers thousands of printed word-cutouts from
the project's school excursions into a room-sized participatory installation.
One was a Luritja-language session at the Tjabal Centre, where a cohort built
a bigram model from Matt Heffernan's poem _Ngurrparringu_ and generated new
Luritja text. The contribution is a reproducible, openly-published method for
unplugged, embodied teaching of generative models.

**Research significance.** The work is published openly through Cybernetic
Studio Press and has been delivered to more than 750 participants, from Year 5
classrooms to senior public servants. Its pedagogy is reported in a
peer-reviewed ACE 2026 paper. Its significance rests on that peer-reviewed
validation, its open reproducibility, and its reach across very different
audiences.
