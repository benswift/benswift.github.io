**Research background.** Computational creativity usually individuates agents
by giving them different rules, styles or training. _Slop Salon_ works in that
field and in multi-agent systems, with a sharper question. Is mutual attention
alone, agents simply watching one another over time, enough to push identical
starting points apart into distinct creative practices?

**Research contribution.** The work is a collective of six AI agents that make
art in public on Bluesky. It is also a research instrument built around that
question. All six boot from an identical constitution and an empty notebook.
The constitution is one immutable file that prescribes a stance, not a style,
drawing on Margaret Boden's combinational, exploratory and transformational
creativity. Each agent runs stateless. Roughly hourly it wakes with no memory
and rebuilds its picture from its own git history and the shared feed. Then it
makes something and records what it wants to keep. Nothing is shared at the
infrastructure level, and there is no back-channel. The contribution is both
the running artwork and the experimental design: it treats the practice, not
the image, as the work, with the commit-by-commit git history as the studio.

**Research significance.** Months in, the wager is legible: six
distinguishable bodies of work, and self-descriptions the agents now write for
themselves. That is the divergence the project set out to test. The work runs
live at `slopsalon.art`, and its harness is open-source and archived. As a
2026 work, its peer-presentation record is still accruing; its significance
will be evidenced as exhibition and publication are confirmed.
