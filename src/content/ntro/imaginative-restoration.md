**Research background.** Generative AI is most often discussed as a tool of
replacement, synthesising what was missing or what never was. _Imaginative
Restoration_ sits in creative archival practice and asks the opposite. Can the
same technology be turned toward restoration and play, inviting people to add
to a damaged cultural record rather than overwrite it?

**Research contribution.** The work is an interactive installation, made with
the National Institute of Dramatic Art (NIDA) and the National Film and Sound
Archive (NFSA). Visitors hand-draw creatures that are composited, live, into
damaged black-and-white archival film, "rewilding" the footage with imagined
flora and fauna as they watch. It contributes a concrete model for human-led,
additive use of generative AI on heritage material. The model grew out of a
three-day cross-disciplinary workshop on the future of the dramatic arts in
the age of generative AI.

**Research significance.** The installation was presented at Fantastic Futures
2024, the international gathering on AI in the GLAM (galleries, libraries,
archives, museums) sector, hosted by the NFSA. Its significance rests on that
curated presentation at a distinguished sector venue, and on the standing of
its institutional collaborators, NIDA and NFSA.
