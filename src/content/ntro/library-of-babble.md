**Research background.** Large language models are widely used and little
understood. Their statistical machinery is hidden behind a chat box: training
as counting, generation as weighted sampling. _The Library of Babble_, part of
_LLMs Unplugged_, works in computing education and public engagement. It asks
how that machinery can be made tangible enough to hold, and whether human scale
changes how people reason about these models.

**Research contribution.** _The Library of Babble_ brings the statistics down
to human scale. It is a growing shelf of pre-trained models in hardbound print.
Each volume typesets the n-gram frequency tables of a single literary work, in
bigram, trigram and 4-gram editions. A reader can hold a whole model in their
hands and generate new text with a die. The contribution is a reproducible,
openly-published method for unplugged, embodied teaching of generative models.

**Research significance.** The work is published openly through Cybernetic
Studio Press and has been delivered to more than 750 participants, from Year 5
classrooms to senior public servants. Its pedagogy is reported in a
peer-reviewed ACE 2026 paper. Its significance rests on that peer-reviewed
validation, its open reproducibility, and its reach across very different
audiences.
