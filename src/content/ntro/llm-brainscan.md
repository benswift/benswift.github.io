**Research background.** Training a language model is normally an opaque
business: you watch a loss curve tick down and hope. _LLM Brainscan_ works in
machine-learning interpretability and data art. It asks what becomes legible
when a model's internal state, mid-training, is shown directly and at full
resolution, not flattened into summary statistics.

**Research contribution.** The work maps every trainable parameter of a small
transformer onto an 8K display: one pixel per weight, around 32 million of
them. The image updates live as the model trains. Attention heads forming,
layer norms stabilising, parts of the network learning at visibly different
rates: all of it becomes directly observable. A microphone makes it
interactive. A visitor's speech steers the output and folds into the training
data. They watch the model train, and become one of the things it trains on.
The contribution is a novel, self-contained instrument for seeing training
dynamics, running on a single compact machine small enough to stand in a
gallery.

**Research significance.** The work is installed in the Cybernetic Studio
(Birch building) and is a citable, openly-archived research output. As a 2026
work, its public-presentation record is still accruing; its significance will
be evidenced as exhibition and peer engagement are confirmed.
