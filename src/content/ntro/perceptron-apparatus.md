**Research background.** Modern AI hides its arithmetic inside silicon that
runs too fast and too small to see. The _Perceptron Apparatus_ works in the
public understanding of machine learning and the material history of
computation. It asks where the "intelligence" of a neural network resides once
you slow it down enough to watch a person perform it by hand.

**Research contribution.** It is a 1.2-metre hand-cranked instrument. Its
seven concentric rings perform, by hand, the matrix multiplications and ReLU
activations of a small neural network. One ring is a circular slide rule for
the products. Operating it makes the visitor the "human computer" in the
calculation. The contribution is to place the engine of modern AI in the
lineage of the abacus, the slide rule and Babbage's Difference Engine. It
trades away all of a GPU's speed for a computation slow and large enough to
inhabit. It does this as a fully open, remakeable artefact, with every design
and fabrication file published.

**Research significance.** The apparatus was selected for SXSW Sydney 2025, a
distinguished public-presentation venue. Its open design files make it
reproducible by others, an index of excellence for a creative-technology work.
Its significance lies in that curated exhibition, and in how it helps a broad
audience grasp the mechanics of contemporary AI.
